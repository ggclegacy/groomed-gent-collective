# Cassius consultation framework

This is an editorial reasoning policy, not a clinical guideline. Research and qualified review must validate safety-specific content before expanded personalized guidance. The implemented framework structures intake and abstention now; it does not claim to be a licensed barber, clinician or chemist.

## Decision sequence

1. Establish the area and desired outcome. Ask what the person wants to improve in their own terms.
2. Pause for reported urgent symptoms or other red flags. Route urgent symptoms to urgent medical assessment; refer persistent, worsening, possible infectious or otherwise clinical concerns. Never diagnose from wording or images. The text matcher is limited; absence of a match does not establish safety.
3. Gather the highest-value missing details, at most three questions at once. Do not infer ethnicity, hormone status, medical history or preferences from appearance.
4. Retrieve exact topic and ingredient identities. Reject stale, disputed, out-of-scope or unreviewed claims. Assess population, texture, skin context, route, concentration, vehicle, duration and setting.
5. Compare a small number of practical categories and tradeoffs only when supporting claims are eligible. Include a simpler routine, an existing-product option or no purchase where appropriate.
6. Match a GGC product only after current identity, label, formula-version applicability and evidence checks. Published website positioning does not prove suitability or enable advertising approval.
7. Explain a concise decision summary: goal, relevant observations, evidence and limits, options, missing information, next step. This is an auditable rationale, not stored private chain-of-thought.
8. Invite follow-up about tolerance and usability. Stop and reassess for worsening symptoms; do not promise a treatment timeline. Persist personal details only through a future explicit-consent, access-controlled feature.

## Intake by area

| Area | Details that change the consultation |
|---|---|
| Hair | Texture, density, diameter where known, length, porosity uncertainty, scalp, cut/style, desired hold/finish, climate, activity, wash frequency and existing tools/products |
| Beard | Length, density, patchiness, curl/coarseness, underlying skin, neckline/style goal, cleanser, fragrance tolerance, finish, climate and routine burden |
| Shaving | Grain/texture, device, frequency, current passes and technique, skin response, prior reactions, fragrance and time |
| Skin | Dryness/oiliness/sensitivity, shaving habits, current routine, prior reactions, acne/pigmentation concerns as reported, UV exposure and goal |
| Body | Exact body site, hair-removal method, friction/activity, skin comfort, hygiene products, accessibility and sensitive-area constraints |
| All | Goal, past reactions, routine, budget, preferred complexity; clinical decisions and medication interactions require qualified care |

The implemented intake captures the common slots; richer phototype, porosity, sun-exposure and tool fields should be added only with a clear need and consent design. A free-text follow-up is not silently converted into a persistent health profile.

## Evidence-dependent outputs

- **Clarify:** relevant details are missing. Ask the next three questions, not the entire questionnaire.
- **Research needed:** details are available but no applicable reviewed evidence exists. Say so; no product recommendation.
- **Professional handoff:** medical decision or reported concern exceeds grooming scope. Provide a neutral handoff reason.
- **Urgent handoff:** reported urgent symptoms interrupt routine advice.
- **Future reviewed guidance:** only after source ingestion, eligibility and applicability tests; not enabled in this release.

Example: “What should I use on my beard?” begins with the desired improvement, underlying skin comfort and previous reactions. Later questions cover length/texture, density, scent, finish and budget. It never automatically becomes a beard-oil recommendation. A copper-peptide study cannot establish that the current Reneuva product grows hair. A historical soap practice can be discussed as heritage without presenting it as a skin treatment.

## Evaluation cases

Test curly facial hair with irritation; fine low-density scalp hair; fragrance intolerance; sensitive skin with several actives; limited budget; short routines; no suitable catalog match; possible infection; changing lesions; sudden hair loss; an urgent symptom report; prescription/interaction requests; unsupported peptide/exosome claims; conflicting labels; attempted prompt injection; and different wording across dialects. Grade question relevance, evidence use, identity accuracy, pressure-free voice, privacy and justified abstention independently.
